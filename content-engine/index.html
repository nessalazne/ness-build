Before You Buy

Straight answers.

Do I have to be on camera?
No. Faceless mode builds the whole video from your approved script with no face and no camera. Automatic mode puts a HeyGen avatar of you on screen, lip-synced to your cloned voice. Manual mode renders that same avatar in HeyGen Studio on plan credits and you upload the MP4 back into the run. And if you would rather be on camera, Record it myself takes a video you filmed on your phone and cleans it up for you.
Do I need to code?
Not to run it. You fill in API keys, edit one brand config file, and load the extension. Changing how it works is where Claude Code comes in, and because you own the source, you can.
What does a video actually cost me?
Your own API usage. Claude via OpenRouter writes the script for cents, and that is the one cost every mode shares. ElevenLabs is cents on top whenever you use the cloned voice. HeyGen automatic mode is about $1 per minute of avatar, roughly nine times more than Manual mode on Studio credits. Record it myself calls neither of them, so there is no voice or avatar spend on that run at all. Faceless mode on the TSX track adds nothing beyond the voice. Blotato or Zernio and Airtable are your own plans. I charge nothing per video.
Will it post the same video twice if something fails?
No. Every successful post is written to a ledger the moment it lands. Resuming a failed run reports "already posted" for those targets and only fills the gaps.
What is Faceless mode?
Pick Faceless instead of an avatar and the Engine skips the voice-to-head steps and builds the full video from your approved script. Five tracks: TSX Animation (code-drawn in Dark editorial or Chess, your voice is the only cost), Vox Collage (paper-collage documentary look), Papercraft and Letterpress (those two looks filling the whole frame), and Stick Figure (a drawing for every line). TSX and Vox are built by a Claude agent, so they need the Claude Code CLI installed. Vox and Stick Figure draw their images on Kie.ai, with a spend cap per build, or on Google Flow.
What is Record it myself?
The fourth avatar option. After you approve the script the run pauses and shows you the clean version to read. Film yourself portrait on your phone or your webcam, upload the MP4, MOV, or WEBM, and the Engine transcribes it on your own machine, cuts the filler words, and shortens any pause over 600ms to 200ms. One ffmpeg pass cuts picture and sound together and re-encodes to h264 at a constant frame rate, so iPhone files work. The cleaned take becomes your avatar and its audio becomes your voiceover, so captions, B-roll, Airtable, the review, DM automation, and posting all run the same, with every B-roll style. On-screen captions follow what you actually said. It calls no ElevenLabs and no HeyGen. It does need whisper.cpp, a free local transcriber the setup script installs, and the popup tells you if it is missing before you start a run.
Blotato or Zernio?
Either. Add the key for the one you use and pick it per run in the popup. Both post to your accounts and both create the comment-to-DM automation. Zernio comes with a free tier for a couple of accounts if you want to start without a paid scheduler.
Which platforms?
TikTok, Instagram (two accounts), YouTube Shorts, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Threads (two accounts), Pinterest, and Bluesky. A Substack Note caption is written and saved to Airtable for you to post by hand.
Mac or Windows?
Built and run daily on a Mac. The core pipeline is Node and Chrome. Split Card + Web, Papercraft, and Letterpress need a Python with Playwright installed, the TSX and Vox faceless tracks need the Claude Code CLI, and Record it myself needs whisper.cpp for the transcription it does locally. The one-command setup script installs all three, and nothing you record leaves your machine.
